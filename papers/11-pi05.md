## Summary

π0.5 studies generalization to new homes through a heterogeneous training recipe that combines robot and broader vision-language data. Its design combines high-level task inference with low-level action generation and uses both discrete and continuous action representations across training stages. The evaluation tests multi-stage household behavior and ablates training ingredients.

## Important parts to learn

- **MUST LEARN: a precise definition of unseen-environment generalization.**
- **MUST LEARN: co-training and hierarchical task decomposition.**
- **ADVANCED: discrete action representations versus continuous flow matching.**

## Where to focus your reading

Sections IV-B–IV-D and V-B–V-E. Focus on the co-training and high-level inference ablations.

**Prerequisites:** VLA architecture, action chunking, transfer learning, and flow matching.

## Small exercise — suggested, not executed

Design a held-out-home test without collecting a huge dataset. State what changes between training and testing, what remains shared, and how partial task completion is scored.

## Critical reading

My assessment: 'open-world' describes the tested generalization setting, not unlimited reliability. Trace claims to the actual task rubric and data splits.
