## Summary

The article surveys AI-assisted space planning and emerging robotic systems, distinguishing current supervised experiments from more ambitious autonomous operations. Delayed communication can make local decisions necessary, while microgravity changes the physical assumptions learned from terrestrial demonstrations. The reporting highlights limited deployment data and staged increases in autonomy.

## Takeaways and important parts to learn

- **MUST LEARN: planning assistance versus autonomous execution.** Identify who checks a plan and which system issues commands.
- **MUST LEARN: distribution shift in dynamics.** A controller trained under gravity may face different motion and contact in orbit.
- **TAKEAWAY: autonomy needs an operating envelope.** My interpretation: specify permitted actions, monitoring, and recovery conditions before evaluating a learned planner.

## Focus while reading

Why Spacecraft Need True Autonomy; Adapting Robotics to Microgravity Physics.

## Suggested learning activity

Compare moving a cargo bag on Earth and in microgravity. Sketch changed forces, observations, and failure modes; propose a simulation test for one difference.

## Evidence and limitations

Proposed missions and company development plans are prospective. The examples do not establish broadly reliable autonomous spacecraft control.

Summary reflects the reporting; learning activities and interpretations are editorial suggestions, not completed experiments.
