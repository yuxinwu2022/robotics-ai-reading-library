# Atlas Robot’s New Hand May Outperform Humanlike Designs

- **Publication:** IEEE Spectrum
- **Published:** 2026-10-01 (verified from publisher metadata)
- **Edition / checked:** 2026-10-05 · America/Chicago
- **Level:** Beginner → intermediate
- **Why selected:** A concrete example of turning manipulation requirements into mechanical design choices.

## Summary

IEEE Spectrum describes Boston Dynamics' redesigned Atlas hand: three fingers and a thumb, 13 degrees of freedom, and replaceable actuator units. The reporting emphasizes capability, repairability, and scalable manufacture. It illustrates how useful motions and contact behavior can matter more than close anatomical imitation.

## Takeaways and important parts to learn

- **MUST LEARN: degrees of freedom versus useful task capability.** List the motions a task requires before choosing a hand layout.
- **MUST LEARN: backdrivability, actuation, and maintainability.** Connect mechanical choices to contact behavior and servicing.
- **TAKEAWAY: optimize the whole system.** My interpretation: a hand should be evaluated with its controller, data needs, cost, and lifetime.

## Focus while reading

The discussion of finger splay, actuators, and production tradeoffs.

## Suggested learning activity

Make a requirements table for grasping a screwdriver and operating a trigger. Compare a two-finger gripper, a four-finger hand, and a five-finger hand.

## Evidence and limitations

The article reports design intentions and manufacturer statements; it does not establish comparative lifetime or performance across all competing hands.

Summary reflects the reporting; learning activities and interpretations are editorial suggestions, not completed experiments.
